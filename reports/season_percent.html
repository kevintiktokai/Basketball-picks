A season from $10,000 at 1% or 2% a bet

Every bet stakes the same share of the account, worked out each morning from the current balance. Stakes grow after winning days and shrink after losing ones. A busy slate never puts more than the whole balance in play: past that, each bet is scaled down. Before you pick 1% or 2%

  • 1% of the account is close to the 1/8 Kelly stake we tested. 2% is about a quarter Kelly: it roughly doubles the growth in good seasons and doubles the drops too.
  • Big slates: the straight bets can produce 50–66 picks on one Saturday. At 1% that puts half to two thirds of the account in play that day; at 2% the day is capped at the whole balance about once a season.
  • The largest figures come from compounding into stakes books will not take. With a $500 limit per bet the 2% upside roughly halves; at 1% the limit barely matters in the first season.
  • If you meant 1% of the starting $10,000, a flat $100 a bet, that is the $100 view of the season planner: a typical +$7,400 from straight bets and +$10,400 with the daily card.
  • About 1 season in 10 ends below $10,000, mostly lean years like 2024-25. Prices assume each game's best opening number across books, bet soon after the line appears.
